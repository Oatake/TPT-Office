## Page 2 — Teacher Guide: How to Use

**Title:** How to Use This Resource

**What's inside**
- Student Helper card (p. 4) — two copies per page. Cut in half and glue into math notebooks.
- 16 student pages (pp. 5–20) at three levels, plus challenge pages.
- Regroup or Not? cut-and-paste sort (pp. 10–11).
- Spot the Mistake error analysis (pp. 14–15).
- Word problems (pp. 16–17) and exit tickets (p. 20).
- Answer keys (pp. 21–26).

**What the shapes mean**
Each student page has a small shape in the top corner. The shapes help you group students without labels like "easy" or "hard."

| Shape | Level | What students do |
|---|---|---|
| ○ circle | Level 1 | Subtract with **no regrouping**. |
| □ square | Level 2 | Subtract **with regrouping** on every problem. |
| △ triangle | Level 3 | **Mixed** problems. Students decide first: regroup or not? |
| ☆ star | Challenge | 3-digit subtraction, missing digits, two-step word problems. |

**Suggested sequence (about 6–8 days, 15–20 minutes per page)**

| Day | Pages | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | p. 4 Student Helper + p. 5 ○ | Subtract the ones, then the tens (no regrouping) |
| Day 2 | p. 6 ○ + p. 7 □ | No-regrouping fluency; trade a ten with base-ten drawings |
| Day 3 | p. 8 □ | Regrouping practice |
| Day 4 | p. 9 + pp. 10–11 △ | Decide first: regroup or not? Sort the cards. |
| Day 5 | p. 12 + p. 13 △ | Check by adding; Solve & Shade |
| Day 6 | p. 14 + p. 15 △ | Spot the Mistake and explain |
| Day 7 | p. 16 + p. 17 + p. 20 | Word problems; exit tickets |
| Any day after Day 4 | pp. 18–19 ☆ | Challenge for students who are ready |

Add a day of review if students need more time with regrouping (pp. 7–8).

**Differentiation tips**
- Level 1 students can join the mixed pages. Assign **only problems 1–2** on pp. 14, 16, and 17, and **only problem 1** on p. 15. These problems do not need regrouping.
- Use the exit tickets (p. 20) to form small groups for the next day.
- Problem 4 on p. 17 is a two-step challenge (☆). It is optional.

**Printing and prep**
- Print **one-sided**. Page 10 (cards) must be on its own sheet, separate from page 11 (sort mat). Do not print double-sided.
- Everything prints well in black and white.
- **No scissors?** Students can skip cutting. They write **R** (regroup) or **N** (no regrouping) next to each card on page 10, then copy the problems onto the sort mat.

**A note about strategies**
Base-ten drawings are always OK. The standard algorithm for subtraction is not required in 2nd grade (Common Core places it in 4.NBT.B.4). The vertical problems here are a written method. Let students show their thinking with drawings, number lines, or adding up, and connect each strategy to place value.

**Vocabulary**
We use **regroup** and **trade a ten** on every student page. We do not use "borrow," because nothing is given back. Key words: subtract, difference, tens, ones, regroup, trade a ten, place value, check.

**Extension ideas**
- **Whiteboards:** Write a problem. Students show a thumbs-up for "regroup" or thumbs-down for "no regrouping" before they solve.
- **Small group:** Use the Student Helper and real base-ten blocks with p. 7.
- **Math center:** Laminate the sort cards (p. 10) and mat (p. 11) for a reusable center.
- **Make your own mistake:** Students write a "Spot the Mistake" problem for a partner.

## Page 4 — Student Helper: Regroup or Not?

> Layout: การ์ดเหมือนกัน 2 ใบ (ครึ่งบน / ครึ่งล่าง) มีเส้นตัดแนวนอนตรงกลาง ข้อความด้านล่างคือเนื้อหา **1 ใบ**

**Card title:** Regroup or Not?

**Steps**
1. Look at the ones.
2. Are there enough ones to take away?
3. No? Trade 1 ten for 10 ones.
4. Subtract the ones.
5. Subtract the tens.
6. Check: add up!

**Example: 52 − 27**

[IMAGE: Base-ten drawing, original SVG. Left panel "Start": 5 rods + 2 units. Middle panel "Trade a ten": 4 rods + 12 units (one rod drawn with dashed outline and an arrow to 10 new units). Right panel "Take away": 4 rods + 12 units with 7 units crossed out (X) and 2 rods crossed out. Remaining: 2 rods + 5 units.]

Vertical work beside the drawing:
```
      4  12        ← small regroup digits written above
      5̶   2̶
   −  2   7
   --------
      2   5
```
- Markup: the 5 is crossed out with a small 4 above it; the 2 is crossed out with a small 12 above it.

Short captions under the example:
- 2 ones are not enough to take away 7.
- Trade 1 ten. Now there are 4 tens and 12 ones.
- 12 − 7 = 5.  4 − 2 = 2.
- **52 − 27 = 25**
- Check: 25 + 27 = 52 ✓

**Tiny rhyme at the bottom (optional):** "Look at the ones before you begin!"

> Writer check (not printed): 52 − 27 → ones 2 < 7 → **regroup YES**. 12 − 7 = 5, 4 − 2 = 2 → 25. Round 2: 25 + 27 = 52 ✓. โจทย์นี้ไม่ซ้ำกับหน้าอื่น

**Page 15 — Spot the Mistake: Explain It △**

1. **Zara, 79 − 34 → correct answer 45.**
   Sample: The mistake is she regrouped when she did not need to. I know because 9 ones is enough to take away 4 ones. 9 − 4 = 5 and 7 − 3 = 4, so 79 − 34 = 45.

2. **Sam, 81 − 47 → correct answer 34.**
   Sample: There were not enough ones, so he traded a ten. The mistake is he did not change the 8 tens to 7 tens. 11 − 7 = 4 and 7 − 4 = 3, so 81 − 47 = 34.

3. **Prove it: 42 − 15 → YES, it needs regrouping. 42 − 15 = 27.**
   Sample: I know because 42 has only 2 ones, and I need to take away 5 ones. I trade 1 ten for 10 ones, so I have 3 tens and 12 ones. 12 − 5 = 7 and 3 − 1 = 2.
