Teacher Guide

The big idea

In Word Rockets, every two-syllable word is a two-stage rocket: students find where it splits, read each stage, and then “blast off” by reading the whole word.

Oral warm-up (1–2 minutes before any page)

Say a word and clap each part: sun-set (2 claps), cat (1 clap), nap-kin (2 claps), splash (1 clap), mit-ten (2 claps). Ask: “How many parts did you hear?” Then show the printed word and find the vowels together.

The routine (use it on every page)

  1. Find the vowels. Put a dot under each vowel.
  2. Split it. Draw one line where the word splits.
  3. Read each part.
  4. Blast off! Read the whole word. Make it sound like a real word.

Marking convention

One dot under every vowel and one vertical line at the split. In print, the split is shown with a line and a space on each side: nap | kin.

Suggested sequence (9 days, 15–20 minutes a day)

Phonics notes for teachers

  1. One vowel letter, one vowel sound. Every word in this resource is made of closed syllables, so each vowel letter stands for one short vowel sound. Later, students will meet words like cake and rain, where two vowel letters make one sound, so keep the language “every syllable has one vowel sound.”
  2. The second syllable may sound lighter. In natural speech, kit | ten sounds more like “kit-n.” That is why step 4 says “Make it sound like a real word.” Students read each part with a short vowel, then adjust to the word they know.
  3. Why there is no rocket, planet, comet, robot, or lemon. These words do not follow the closed-syllable VC/CV pattern. Robot splits after the vowel (ro | bot, an open syllable), planet and lemon split after the consonant (plan | et, lem | on), and rocket has the digraph ck, which never splits. These are the next patterns to teach, so they appear only in the pictures and in teacher text.
  4. 2nd grade review. Use the Comet pages (9, 10, 13 spaces 14–27) and the Mission Check as a quick screener. On pages 11 and 17, ask students how the meanings of the two small words help them predict the meaning of a compound word (CCSS L.2.4.d).

Teacher Prep Guide: Games & Mini-Book

1. How to play Race to the Moon (pp. 12–13)

  • Players: 2–4. Time: 10–15 minutes. Use in literacy centers, small groups, as an early-finisher task, or send home for family play.
  • You need: one board, one paper clip, one pencil, and a game piece for each player (cut from the Flight Log, p. 14, or use erasers or coins).
  • To play: Players start on Earth. Spin and move that many spaces. Read the word on your space with the routine: split it, read each part, blast off (read the whole word). If the group agrees, stay. If not, check the Split Check strip. If the reading was wrong, go back 1 space.
  • Boost and Meteor: Boost = go ahead 2 spaces. Meteor = go back 1 space. After a Boost or Meteor move, read the word on your new space.
  • Winning: The first player to reach the Moon wins. An exact spin is not needed.
  • Flight Log: Each player records the words they land on (up to 6), then circles the hardest word. Collect the logs as evidence of learning.
  • Board A or B? Board A (compound words) for the Moon group. Board B for Planet (spaces 1–13 are VC/CV words with no blends) and Comet (spaces 14–27 have blends and digraphs).
  • Variations: Partner reading (one partner reads, the other checks with the strip, then switch). Whole-class play: project the board with a document camera and play in two teams.

2. Making the spinner

Put the pencil tip through one end of a paper clip, hold the pencil straight up on the center dot, and flick the clip.

3. The Split Check strip

Fold the strip under the board along the fold line before play. Players open it only when the group is not sure about a word.

4. Folding the mini-book (p. 15)

Before you start, turn the page sideways so that page 1 (the cover) is right side up in the bottom-right box.

  1. Fold the page in half the long way (top edge to bottom edge). Crease and unfold.
  2. Fold it in half the short way. Crease and unfold.
  3. Fold both short ends in to meet the middle crease. Crease and unfold. You now see 8 boxes.
  4. Fold the page in half the short way again. Cut along the cut line from the folded edge to the next crease (the cut goes only across the two middle boxes).
  5. Open the page and fold it the long way again. Hold the two ends and push them toward the middle so the cut opens up.
  6. Keep pushing until the pages fold flat into a little book. Turn it so the cover (page 1) is on the front. Crease well.

No-fold option: Cut the 8 boxes apart, stack the pages in order 1–8 (page numbers are in the corners), and staple on the left.

5. Printing tips

  • Print page 15 one-sided at Actual size / 100% so the fold lines line up.
  • Print the game boards on cardstock or slide them into sheet protectors to reuse them.
  • Do not print pages 6 and 7 double-sided (students cut them).

6. No-scissors options

  • Page 6: Students write the matching word part in each docking port instead of gluing.
  • Page 7: Students write 1 or 2 on each card instead of cutting and gluing.
