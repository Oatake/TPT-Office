## Page 2 — Teacher Guide & What's Inside

**Title:** Teacher Guide & What's Inside

### About This Resource
This no-prep set helps third graders see fractions as numbers on a number line. Students partition 0 to 1 into equal parts, then name and place unit fractions and fractions a/b, including fractions greater than 1. They also find equivalent fractions and whole numbers at the same point. Every page uses one simple story: Juniper the snowshoe hare hops from Base Camp (0) to the Cocoa Cabin (1), and every hop on a trail is the same size.

### The Trail Routine (use it every day)
1. **Count the hops.** How many equal parts (intervals) are there from 0 to 1? That number is the **denominator**.
2. **Name one hop.** Each hop is the **unit fraction** 1/b.
3. **Hop to the spot.** Start at 0 and count hops. The number of hops is the **numerator**.

Always pair the trail word with the math word: "each hop (equal part)," "the cabin (1 whole)."

### What the Paws Mean
Each student page has 1, 2, or 3 pairs of paw prints in the top corner. The paws let you group students without labels like "easy" or "hard."

| Paws | Level | Best for | What students do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 pair | Base Camp | New learners, intervention | Unit fractions on 0–1. Tick marks are given or guided by dots; some hop arcs are drawn. |
| 2 pairs | Trail | Most of the class | Any fraction a/b on 0–1, plus some fractions greater than 1 on 0–2 lines. |
| 3 pairs | Summit | Students ready for a challenge | Equivalent fractions, whole numbers as fractions, 0–3 lines, and written proof. |

**Differentiate fast:** All three Trail Mazes use the same rules, so you can give directions once. Then hand out Maze 1 (Base Camp), Maze 2 (Trail), or Maze 3 (Summit). Pages 4 and 5 get harder as students go down the page, so Base Camp students may stop halfway. Pages 8, 10, and 11 include sentence frames for English learners and reluctant writers.
**Early finishers:** On the back of page 8, students design a "trick" trail sign for a friend to check.

### Suggested 6-Day Path

| Day | Pages | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 Trail Guide → 4 Mark the Trail → Ticket A | Partition 0 to 1; unit fractions (3.NF.A.2a) |
| 2 | 5 Footprint Fractions → 6 Trail Maze 1 | Name and place 1/b and a/b |

| 5 | 10 Fix the Trail Sign → 11 Trail Talk | Error analysis and reasoning |

Most pages take 15–25 minutes; the game takes about 20 minutes. Every page also works as a math center or small-group task.

### Tips
1. **Keep the Trail Guide (p. 3) close.** Students can keep a copy in a folder or post it as an anchor chart.
2. **"Count the hops, not the marks."** A line cut into 4 hops has 5 tick marks. This is the most common number line error, and Mazes 1 and 2 include it as a trap.
3. **Mazes check themselves.** Each Cocoa Cabin says how many footprints the right trail has. If a student hits a dead end or the count is wrong, they go back to the last sign they colored and re-check it. The answer key (p. 16) shows which mistake each wrong sign matches.
4. **Name the point with the line's hops.** On a fourths line the answer is 2/4. Students do not need to rewrite fractions with smaller numbers, because that is not a grade 3 standard. If a student writes 1/2 and explains that it is the same point, accept it.

### Main example

[IMAGE: Juniper (#3, pointing pose) standing on the footprint at 3/4, pointing back toward 0.]

**Callout 1 — Count the hops. → the denominator**
There are 4 equal hops (equal parts) from 0 to 1. The **denominator** is 4.

**Callout 2 — Name one hop. → the unit fraction**
Each hop is 1/4 of the whole. 1/4 is a **unit fraction**.

**Callout 3 — Hop to the spot. → the numerator**
Start at 0. Juniper took 3 hops of 1/4. The **numerator** is 3. She is at **3/4**.

### Box: Count the hops, not the marks!
`NL[0–1, 4, point=—, labels=0,1]`, about 3.5 in long. Under the 5 tick marks are small gray numbers 1 2 3 4 5 with a berry line crossing them out, plus the crossed-out note: "5 marks → 1/5". Above the line are 4 hop arcs numbered 1 2 3 4, with the note: "4 hops → fourths."
Text: **0 is where Juniper starts. She has not hopped yet. 4 hops make 5 marks. Count the hops!**

### Box: You reached the cabin!
`NL[0–1, 4, point=1, labels=0,1]`, about 3 in long, with four hop arcs and a footprint at the cabin.
Text: **4 hops of 1/4 reach the cabin. 4/4 = 1 whole.**

[IMAGE: Small Cocoa Cabin (#7) beside this box.]

### Word Bank
| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| **number line** | A line that shows numbers in order. |
| **fraction** | A number that names equal parts of a whole. |
| **numerator** | The top number. It counts hops from 0. |
| **denominator** | The bottom number. Equal hops from 0 to 1. |
| **equal parts** | Parts that are all the same size. |
| **unit fraction** | One equal part, like 1/4. |
| **whole** | The whole trail from 0 to 1. |
| **tick mark** | A short line that marks a spot. |
| **interval** | The space between two tick marks. One hop! |
| **equivalent** | Equal. Worth the same amount. |
| **same point** | One spot on the line with two names. |

*(Every definition is 10 words or fewer.)*

## Maze build notes (apply to pp. 6, 7, 9; not printed)

- **Grid:** 4 columns × 5 rows. R1C1 is top-left and R5C4 is bottom-right. **Base Camp** (#6) sits above R1C1, and the **Cocoa Cabin** (#7) sits below R5C4. The self-check box goes beside the Cabin.
- **One true path only.** For every maze, the set of TRUE cells is exactly the path, and I checked every pair of true cells: the only true cells that touch (up/down/left/right) are the ones next to each other on the path. So there is exactly one route from Base Camp to the Cabin, and it has no shortcuts.
- **Can a 4 × 5 grid meet every plan rule? (proof summary).** I listed every self-avoiding, non-touching path from R1C1 to R5C4 by working backward from the Cabin.
  - **10 cells:** only 3 such paths exist. Only one of them (used for Maze 1) has no turn in a corner cell.
  - **12 cells:** only 2 such paths exist, and **both** turn in a corner (R1C4 or R5C1).
  - A corner cell has only two neighbors, and both are on the path, so the plan's rule 4 ("a false cell beside every turn") **cannot** be met at that one corner turn.
  - **Fix used:** In Maze 2 (corner R1C4) and Maze 3 (corner R5C1), the cell just before the corner has a strong false neighbor instead. Every other turn has at least one false neighbor. Maze 3 uses a different path from Maze 1, so students cannot copy the shape from page 6.
- **Cell drawing:** trail-sign frame (#9), a number line about 1.4 in long, labels 0 and 1 (plus 2 or 3 on longer lines) at 12 pt or larger, a fraction at 16 pt bold, and a 0.22 in empty circle to color. Draw faint footprints between neighboring cells.
- **Footprint marker:** use a filled footprint (#5) or a filled circle at least 0.12 in on the point.

**3.NF.A — Develop understanding of fractions as numbers.**
